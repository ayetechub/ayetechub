10 Coohom Tips Every Interior Designer Must Know in 2026

📅 June 5, 2026 ✍ Awet G. Nway 🕑 8 min read
🌐 Read in:

Coohom has become one of the fastest-growing interior design platforms globally — and for good reason. It combines an intuitive 3D modelling environment with a massive furniture catalogue, photorealistic rendering, and powerful presentation tools, all accessible directly in your browser. But like any professional design software, there is a significant gap between knowing where the buttons are and using the platform in a way that consistently produces stunning, client-ready results.

These 10 tips come from working with Coohom across residential, commercial, and hospitality projects. Some are workflow habits, some are hidden features, and some are common mistakes that cost beginners hours of rework. Whether you are starting your interior design journey or looking to level up your professional output, these tips will change how you use Coohom.

"In interior design, the difference between a good design and a great design is often not the furniture — it is the light, the proportion, and the way the space feels when someone walks into it. Coohom gives you the tools to design all three before you spend a single dollar."
1Always Set Up Your Project Correctly Before Drawing

The single most common beginner mistake in Coohom is jumping straight into drawing walls without configuring the project first. Spend 3 minutes on setup — it saves 30 minutes of rework later.

Before drawing your first wall, configure:

  • Unit system: Go to Settings → Units. Choose mm or cm for your working region. Changing units after drawing walls causes scaling errors.
  • Storey height: Set your ceiling height in the Storey panel (typically 2,800–3,200mm for residential). Every wall you draw will inherit this height.
  • Grid spacing: Set the snap grid to 100mm or 50mm depending on your project complexity. The snap grid is your best friend for accurate drawing.
  • Wall thickness defaults: Set your default wall thickness (external: 200–350mm, internal: 100–150mm) before drawing. Changing thickness after placement requires selecting and editing each wall individually.
💡 Pro Move

Import your site plan as a background image and scale it to the correct dimensions using the reference measurement tool. Now draw walls directly over the plan — guaranteed accurate dimensions from the start.

2Master the Catalogue Search to Find Furniture Faster

Coohom's catalogue has millions of 3D models. Scrolling through categories is the slowest possible way to find what you need. Professional designers use search and filters to locate the exact piece in under 60 seconds.

  • Use specific keywords: Instead of "sofa", search "3-seater modular sofa grey fabric" — the more specific the search, the more relevant the results.
  • Filter by brand: If you have a preferred brand (IKEA, Muuto, Minotti, etc.), filter by brand to find exact catalogue items for realistic pricing conversations with clients.
  • Filter by dimensions: Enter maximum width and depth constraints to find furniture that will physically fit your space before placing it.
  • Use colour filters: Filter by colour family when you have an established palette — avoids placing pieces that then need extensive recolouring.
  • Save favourites: Click the heart icon on any piece you use regularly. Your Favourites folder becomes your personal curated catalogue — much faster than searching from scratch for every project.
3Use the Measurement Tool Constantly — Not Just for Checking

Most beginners use the measurement tool only to check dimensions after placing furniture. Professional designers use it while placing to ensure every piece is in its exact intended position the first time.

  • Check clearances: Before finalising a furniture layout, measure every walking path. Minimum 900mm for primary circulation, 600mm for secondary access. Kitchen work triangle: maximum 7m perimeter.
  • Verify sofa-to-TV distance: Optimal viewing distance = screen diagonal × 1.5–2.5. A 65" TV (165cm diagonal): optimal distance 250–415cm. Measure it in Coohom before your client asks.
  • Check door swing clearances: Place a door, then measure how far the swing protrudes into the room. Many beginners place furniture that a door would hit — embarrassing when a client spots it.
  • Bedside tables: Minimum 500mm walkway beside a bed, ideally 600–750mm. Measure both sides — asymmetric room layouts often create a tight side that gets overlooked.
4Lighting Is the Most Underused Feature in Coohom

The single biggest difference between an amateur Coohom render and a professional one is lighting. Beginners use the default daylight setting and wonder why their renders look flat. Professionals layer multiple light sources to create depth, mood, and focus.

  • Start with natural light: Set your exterior sun position (time of day, latitude, season) to get the right quality of incoming light. Morning light is warm and directional; midday is harsh; late afternoon is golden.
  • Add ambient light for fill: Raise ambient light to 20–30% to prevent overly dark shadows in areas not directly lit. Real rooms have reflected light from walls, floors, and ceilings — replicate this.
  • Place point lights for warmth: Add point lights inside lampshades and floor lamps. Set colour temperature to 2700–3000K for warm residential feel, 4000K for contemporary spaces, 5000K for offices.
  • Use spotlight for focal points: Add downlights above artwork, kitchen islands, and feature walls. Position them at the correct ceiling height (typically 450–600mm from the feature).
  • Turn off default sunlight for evening renders: Create a separate "Evening Scene" with all interior lights on and exterior light off — stunning for client presentations of moody spaces.
⚠ Lighting Mistake to Avoid

Never render a room with only the default global illumination enabled. The flat, uniform lighting produces renders that look like a furniture catalogue, not a designed space. Always add a minimum of 3 intentional light sources per room.

5Apply Materials at the Right Level — Room vs Object

Coohom allows you to apply materials to individual objects, to whole walls, or to the entire room floor/ceiling. Knowing the difference between these levels and when to use each saves enormous time.

  • Room-level materials: Right-click the room → Apply material to floor/ceiling. This applies a single material across the entire surface — fastest for uniform finishes like hardwood floors or white ceilings.
  • Wall-level materials: Click a wall → Paint bucket icon → apply material. All faces of that specific wall get the material. Use for feature walls and accent colours.
  • Face-level (single surface): In 3D view, click directly on the surface → paint bucket → apply. Use this for half-painted walls, tiled bath areas up to a specific height, or panelling on the lower half of a wall.
  • Object-level recolouring: Click any furniture piece → Material tab → replace any individual component material. This is how you change the fabric colour of a sofa while keeping its legs the original finish.
  • Material scale adjustment: After applying a tile or pattern, always check the scale. A 300×300mm bathroom tile should tile 3–4 times per metre — if it looks like one giant tile covering the wall, reduce the scale to 30–35%.
6Use Smart Grouping and Alignment for Professional Layouts

Perfectly aligned furniture is what separates a designed space from a randomly arranged room. Coohom has alignment tools that most designers never discover because they are hidden in the right-click menu.

  • Group related furniture: Select multiple pieces → Right-click → Group. Move or rotate the group together. Essential for dining table sets, bed + nightstands, sofa arrangements.
  • Align to wall: Select a piece → Right-click → Align → Align to Wall. The piece snaps flush against the selected wall face, perfectly parallel and touching.
  • Distribute evenly: Select 3+ objects → Right-click → Distribute Horizontally or Vertically. Spaces them perfectly evenly — no manual measuring. Use for picture rails, chairs around a table, shelving units.
  • Copy and paste in-place: Select an object → Ctrl+C → Ctrl+V. The copy appears in the same position. Then move it to the new location. Faster than dragging from the catalogue for the same piece.
  • Mirror horizontally: For symmetrical bedroom layouts, place and perfect one bedside table arrangement, then mirror it to the other side. Half the work, perfect symmetry.
7Camera Angles Make or Break Your Renders

The same room can look spectacular or mediocre depending entirely on the camera position, height, and field of view. Professional interior photographers know this — so should digital designers.

  • Camera height for residential: Set camera eye level to 1,400–1,600mm. This replicates a standing adult's natural eye level — the most flattering height for most residential spaces.
  • Use 3-point composition: Position the camera so you see three walls (two side walls and the far wall). This creates depth and makes rooms appear larger than straight-on shots.
  • Wide angle for small rooms: Reduce field of view to 60–75° for small rooms to capture more of the space. Avoid going wider than 75° — distortion makes furniture look warped.
  • Save multiple camera positions: Coohom allows you to save named camera positions. Save: "Living Room Entrance View", "Sofa Zone", "Kitchen from Dining" — then clients can navigate your designs independently.
  • Level your horizon: A tilted camera horizon (where the vertical lines of walls are not truly vertical) looks deeply unprofessional. Always keep the camera level unless intentionally going for a dramatic angle.
8Render at the Right Settings for Your Purpose

Coohom offers different render quality levels. Using the highest quality for every iteration wastes hours. Using low quality for client deliverables looks unprofessional. Know which to use when.

  • Real-time (draft) rendering: Use during design to check proportions, colours, and lighting setup. Instant. This is NOT for client sharing.
  • Standard render (720p, medium quality): Use for internal review and early concept presentation. Renders in 3–10 minutes. Good enough for first-round client feedback.
  • High quality render (1080p full HD): Use for final client presentations, proposal documents, and social media posts. Renders in 15–45 minutes depending on complexity. This is the minimum for professional deliverables.
  • Ultra HD render (4K): Use only for final approved designs going to print, large-format display, or premium client packages. Can take 1–3 hours per image.
  • Render multiple angles in one batch: Set up all your saved camera positions → Render → Batch render. Submit all renders overnight and wake up to a complete set of finished images.
💡 Time Saving Tip

Always finalise your lighting, materials, and furniture before starting a high-quality render. One change after a 45-minute render means another 45-minute render. Lock the design first, render second.

9Create Professional Client Presentations Inside Coohom

Most designers export their renders and then build presentations in PowerPoint or Canva. Coohom has a built-in presentation module that many users never discover — it is faster and produces more impressive results than any external tool.

  • Use the Panorama view: From any camera position, click Panorama → Generate 360° view. Share a link with your client. They can rotate and look around the room on any device, in any direction. This single feature is more impressive than any static render.
  • Virtual walkthrough: Set up multiple camera positions as "waypoints" → Create Walkthrough → Share link. Clients walk through the design themselves, in their own time. Removes you from the presentation loop.
  • Floorplan + 3D split view: Create a split-screen view showing the floor plan alongside the 3D render of the same room. Helps clients understand spatial relationships they cannot grasp from 3D alone.
  • Material and furniture schedule: Coohom generates an automatic Bill of Materials (BOM) listing every item placed in the design with its name, brand, and quantity. Export this as a client specification document or for procurement.
  • Custom branding: Add your studio logo and branding to the Coohom presentation link. Under Account Settings, upload your logo — it appears in the header of all shared presentations. Professional first impression.
10Learn the Keyboard Shortcuts — Double Your Speed

Experienced Coohom users work significantly faster than beginners not because they know more features — but because they never take their hands off the keyboard. These shortcuts eliminate 80% of menu navigation.

DAdd Door opening

Mouse navigation in 3D view: Left-click drag = orbit/rotate. Right-click drag = pan. Scroll wheel = zoom in/out. Middle-click drag = pan (same as right-click drag). These five mouse gestures cover 95% of 3D navigation — practise until they are instinct.

Putting It All Together — A Professional Workflow

The most efficient Coohom workflow follows this sequence: Setup → Floor plan → Walls → Openings → Materials (walls/floor/ceiling) → Furniture placement → Lighting → Camera setup → Render → Present. Working in this order prevents the most common rework cycles. Adding furniture before materials means you often need to move furniture to see the wall behind it. Adding lighting before furniture means you cannot judge the room's atmosphere correctly.

The designers who produce the most impressive Coohom work are not the ones who know the most advanced features. They are the ones who have a consistent, disciplined process that they follow for every project — and they execute the fundamentals (proportion, light, material, colour) with more care and intentionality than average users apply to their entire designs.
