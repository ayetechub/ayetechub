Avoiding common scams

  • "Too good to be true" pricing. A brand-new phone at half market price is a red flag, not a bargain.
  • Pressure to act immediately. Genuine sellers and buyers rarely need you to decide or pay within minutes.
  • Requests to pay before you've seen the item. Never send money for goods you haven't inspected, unless you fully trust the arrangement and understand the risk.
  • Refusal to meet in person or video call. A seller who avoids any real-time contact is worth being cautious about.
  • Fake shipping/courier deals. Be wary of anyone insisting on a courier or "delivery agent" you didn't choose yourself.

Meeting safely in person

  • Choose a busy, public, well-lit location — a shopping area, mall entrance, or similar.
  • Meet during daylight hours whenever possible.
  • Bring a friend or family member, especially for higher-value items.
  • Tell someone else where you're going and when you expect to be back.
  • Trust your instincts — if something feels wrong, you're allowed to leave.

Verifying a listing

  • Look for multiple, clear, original photos — not stock images pulled from the internet.
  • Read the full description, not just the title.
  • Check whether the seller has a verified badge and how long they've had an account.
  • Ask specific questions only the real owner of the item could answer.
  • For vehicles or property, ask to see supporting documents in person.

Protecting your personal information

  • You never need to share your home address to complete a transaction — meet in public instead.
  • Keep sensitive documents (ID numbers, bank details) out of chat unless you fully understand why they're needed and trust the other party.
  • Use in-app chat instead of sharing your personal phone number if you'd rather not.
  • Be cautious of anyone asking for OTP codes, passwords, or banking PINs — AYE Marketplace or any legitimate seller will never ask for these.

Recognizing fake buyers

Watch for buyers who: overpay and ask for a refund of the difference, claim to be "out of the country" and need a courier, ask you to click a link to "confirm payment," or send screenshots of payment that turn out to be fake. If a buyer's story keeps changing or doesn't quite add up, it's reasonable to walk away.

Recognizing fake sellers

Watch for sellers who: reuse the same photos across multiple unrelated listings, have no verification and a brand-new account, refuse any form of live contact, or ask for a deposit "to hold the item" before you've met. A legitimate seller has nothing to lose by letting you inspect an item first.

Payment safety

  • Inspect the item in person before paying whenever possible.
  • For local mobile-money or bank transfers, confirm the receiving name matches who you're dealing with.
  • Keep a record (screenshot, receipt) of any payment you make.
  • Never send payment through an unfamiliar link — go directly to your bank or mobile money app.

Verified sellers

Sellers can submit a government-issued ID (Fayda ID, Kebele Residence ID, Driver's License, Business License, or Passport) for manual review by our team. Once approved, a verified badge appears on their profile and listings. Verification is a strong trust signal, but always combine it with your own judgment — verification confirms identity, not honesty in every individual transaction.

Community moderation

Listings and reported users are reviewed by our moderation team against our Community Guidelines. Listings that violate our rules — counterfeit goods, prohibited items, spam, or scams — are removed, and repeat violations lead to account suspension.

Reporting & blocking

Every listing and profile has a Report option — use it for anything that looks like a scam, prohibited content, or abusive behavior. You can also block any user directly from their profile or a chat conversation; once blocked, they can no longer message you or see your listings' contact options.

Emergency situations

If you ever feel physically unsafe during or after a meetup, contact local emergency services immediately — that takes priority over anything happening in the app. Afterward, report the user to us so we can investigate and take action on their account.
