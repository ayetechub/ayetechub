Direct Online (DOL) Motor Starter — Control & Power Circuit

The simplest and most common motor starting method. The main contactor connects the motor directly to the supply voltage on pressing START. A normally-closed E-STOP button and overload relay contact provide protection. The holding contact maintains power to the contactor coil after START is released.

📝 Engineering Notes — DOL Starter

  • Application: DOL suitable for motors up to 5.5 kW on 400V systems (starting current = 6–8× FLC). Larger motors require star-delta or VFD.
  • Overload relay setting: Set to motor FLC × 1.05–1.15. Thermal overload trips in ~10 seconds at 150% overload, ~2 hours at 105%.
  • E-STOP wiring: Always wire Emergency Stop button as Normally Closed (NC) — so a broken wire or disconnected terminal is a safe failure (contactor drops out).
  • Holding contact: The KM1 auxiliary NO contact in parallel with START button maintains the control circuit after the START button is released (seal-in or latching contact).
  • Cable sizing: Power cable rated at minimum 1.25 × I_FLC per NEC/IEC standards. Control cable minimum 1.5 mm² for 24V circuits.
  • Standards: IEC 60947-4-1 (contactors & starters), IEC 60947-5-1 (control devices), NFPA 79 (industrial machinery electrical).

Star-Delta (Y-Δ) Motor Starter — Reduced Voltage Starting

Star-delta starting reduces starting current to approximately 33% of DOL starting current (1/3 of line voltage applied to each winding in star connection). Used for motors above 5.5 kW where DOL starting current would cause supply disturbance. Requires a 6-terminal motor.

📝 Engineering Notes — Star-Delta Starter

  • Requirement: Motor must have 6 accessible terminals (U1, V1, W1, U2, V2, W2). Standard cage motors with only 3 terminals cannot be connected star-delta.
  • Timer setting: Transition timer set to 5–10 seconds typically. Motor must reach ~80% of full speed in star before switching to delta. Too fast = current spike on transition; too slow = excessive heating in star.
  • Transition current spike: At the moment of transition from star to delta, a current spike of 4–6× FLC can occur. An autotransformer starter provides smoother transition.
  • Dead-band: The KM_Star contactor must open and fully extinguish the arc before KM_Delta closes — minimum 50–100ms gap. Most modern time relays include this dead-band.
  • Limitation: Not suitable for high-inertia loads (fans, centrifuges) where the motor may not reach sufficient speed in star before delta transition.

Variable Frequency Drive (VFD) Wiring — Power, Control & Safety

A VFD converts fixed AC supply to variable frequency AC output, controlling motor speed precisely. Provides soft start, overload protection, energy savings (affinity law: power ∝ speed³), and integrated motor protection. Most common for pumps, fans, compressors, and conveyors.

📝 Engineering Notes — VFD Wiring

  • No isolation contactor needed: The VFD provides full motor protection. A main MCCB (no fuses on output) is sufficient. Never install a contactor between VFD output and motor that opens during running — this destroys IGBT modules.
  • Cable screening: Use screened (shielded) cable between VFD and motor, especially for cables over 10m. Ground screen at both ends. Separate power and control cables by minimum 300mm.
  • Output reactor: Required for cable runs over 50m to prevent capacitive charging currents from damaging motor insulation. Use dU/dt filter for standard motors not rated for VFD duty.
  • Motor suitability: Motors connected to VFDs should be "inverter duty" rated — class F insulation minimum. Older motors on VFDs may require dU/dt filters to protect insulation.
  • Energy saving — affinity law: Pump/fan power ∝ speed³. Reducing speed to 80% reduces power to 51%. Reducing to 50% reduces power to 12.5%.
  • EMC compliance: Install EMC filter on VFD input per EN 61800-3. Required in most countries for CE marking.

PLC I/O Wiring — Digital Inputs, Digital Outputs & Analog Signals

Correct PLC I/O wiring is fundamental to system reliability. Digital inputs read field device states (sensors, switches). Digital outputs drive actuators (contactors, solenoids, lamps). Analog I/O handles continuous signals (4–20mA, 0–10V). All field devices must be properly sourced, sunk, or shielded.

📝 Engineering Notes — PLC I/O Wiring

  • Sink vs Source (DI): S7-1200 DI inputs are sourcing (PNP) by default. Connect sensor (+) to DI terminal, sensor (−) to M (common). For NPN sensors, use sourcing input in "sink" mode — check CPU wiring diagram.
  • DO load current: S7-1200 transistor outputs rated 0.5A per point, 4A per group. For contactor coils above 0.5A, use an intermediate relay (24VDC coil, contacts to drive the contactor).
  • Analog wiring: Use twisted-pair shielded cable for all analog signals. Ground shield at PLC end only (one-end grounding prevents ground loops). Keep analog cables away from power cables by minimum 300mm.
  • Power supply sizing: Calculate total current: CPU + all I/O modules + field devices powered from PLC. Add 30% margin. Use separate PSU for I/O vs sensors for noise isolation.
  • Protection: Install 1A fuse per group of DI inputs on the +24V feed. Protects against wiring faults in field without shutting down entire PLC power supply.

Safety Circuit — E-Stop Category 0 & LOTO Isolation Points

Emergency stop and lockout/tagout circuits are the most safety-critical wiring in any industrial panel. Designed and wired incorrectly, they create a false sense of security. All safety circuits must be fail-safe: a broken wire, open connection, or component failure must result in the safe state (power removed from actuators).

📝 Engineering Notes — Safety Circuits

  • E-Stop Category 0 vs Category 1: Cat 0 = immediate removal of power (most common). Cat 1 = controlled stop, then power removed. Cat 2 = controlled stop, power maintained for monitoring. Select per ISO 13849/IEC 62061 risk assessment.
  • Force-guided contacts: Safety relays use force-guided (mechanically linked) contacts — if one contact welds closed, all linked contacts are mechanically prevented from opening. This allows the safety relay to detect contact welding on the next reset cycle.
  • Dual-channel wiring (SIL 2): For SIL 2, wire E-stop button through two independent NC contacts (two separate circuits) — the safety relay monitors both channels. If they disagree, the relay detects a fault and prevents restart.
  • Manual reset: After an E-stop activation, a deliberate manual RESET action (separate button) must be required before the machine can restart. This prevents automatic restart after power restoration — a major cause of accidents.
  • LOTO verification: After applying all locks, always press the machine START button to verify the machine cannot start. Test all terminals with a calibrated CAT III meter to confirm zero voltage. This step is non-negotiable.
