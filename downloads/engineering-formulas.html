⚡ Electrical Engineering Formulas
Ohm's Law
V = I × R
Voltage equals current multiplied by resistance. The foundation of all circuit analysis.
V = Voltage (V)  |  I = Current (A)  |  R = Resistance (Ω)
DC & AC circuits
Electrical Power
P = V × I = I²R = V²/R
Power in watts. Three equivalent forms depending on known quantities.
P = Power (W)  |  V = Voltage (V)  |  I = Current (A)
DC circuits
3-Phase Active Power
P = √3 × VL × IL × cos φ
Real power consumed in a balanced 3-phase system. Used for motor and load sizing.
VL = Line voltage (V)  |  IL = Line current (A)  |  cos φ = power factor (0–1)
3-phase AC
3-Phase Apparent Power
S = √3 × VL × IL
Apparent power (VA) — what the supply must provide including reactive power.
S = Apparent power (VA or kVA)
3-phase AC
Power Factor
PF = P / S = cos φ
Ratio of real to apparent power. PF=1 is ideal. Low PF increases current and losses.
P = Real power (W)  |  S = Apparent power (VA)
Dimensionless (0–1)
Capacitive Reactance
Xc = 1 / (2π × f × C)
Impedance of a capacitor at frequency f. Decreases as frequency increases.
f = Frequency (Hz)  |  C = Capacitance (F)
Ohms (Ω)
Inductive Reactance
XL = 2π × f × L
Impedance of an inductor at frequency f. Increases as frequency increases.
L = Inductance (H)  |  f = Frequency (Hz)
Ohms (Ω)
Cable Voltage Drop
ΔV = (2 × L × I × ρ) / A
Voltage drop in a single-phase cable. Limit: 3% for final circuits, 5% overall.
L = Cable length (m)  |  I = Current (A)  |  ρ = 0.0172 (Cu)  |  A = Cross-section (mm²)
Volts (V)
Transformer Turns Ratio
N₁/N₂ = V₁/V₂ = I₂/I₁
Relationship between primary and secondary voltages, currents, and turns in a transformer.
N = Turns  |  V = Voltage  |  I = Current (subscripts 1=primary, 2=secondary)
AC transformers
Short Circuit Current (3-phase)
Isc = V_LL / (√3 × Ztotal)
Fault current at a bus. Used to select circuit breaker interrupting ratings.
V_LL = Line-to-line voltage  |  Z = Total impedance of source + cables (Ω)
Amperes (A)
Motor Full Load Current (3φ)
I_FLC = P / (√3 × V × η × PF)
Full load current of a 3-phase motor. Used for cable sizing and protective device selection.
P = Rated power (W)  |  η = Motor efficiency  |  PF = Power factor
Amperes (A)
Energy Consumption
E = P × t / 1000
Electrical energy consumed. Multiply by tariff rate to get cost.
P = Power (W)  |  t = Time (hours)  |  E = Energy (kWh)
kWh
⚙ Mechanical Engineering Formulas
Force & Newton's 2nd Law
F = m × a
Force equals mass times acceleration. Foundation of dynamics.
F = Force (N)  |  m = Mass (kg)  |  a = Acceleration (m/s²)
Newtons (N)
Mechanical Power
P = F × v = T × ω
Power from force and velocity, or torque and angular velocity.
T = Torque (Nm)  |  ω = Angular velocity (rad/s)  |  v = velocity (m/s)
Watts (W)
Shaft Torque from Motor
T = (P × 9550) / n
Torque at shaft for a motor of power P running at speed n.
P = Power (kW)  |  n = Speed (RPM)  |  T = Torque (Nm)
Newton-metres (Nm)
Stress (Direct)
σ = F / A
Normal stress in a member under axial load. Must be below yield strength.
σ = Stress (Pa)  |  F = Force (N)  |  A = Cross-sectional area (m²)
Pascals (Pa) or N/mm²
Strain & Young's Modulus
E = σ / ε    ε = ΔL / L
Stiffness ratio of stress to strain. Steel: E=200 GPa. Aluminium: E=70 GPa.
ε = Strain (dimensionless)  |  ΔL = elongation  |  L = original length
GPa or N/mm²
Fluid Flow Rate
Q = A × v
Volume flow rate equals cross-sectional area times fluid velocity.
Q = Flow (m³/s)  |  A = Area (m²)  |  v = Velocity (m/s)
m³/s or L/s
Bernoulli Equation
P + ½ρv² + ρgh = constant
Energy conservation in fluid flow. Used for pump sizing and pipe design.
P = Pressure (Pa)  |  ρ = Fluid density (kg/m³)  |  h = Height (m)
Pa (pressure)
Heat Transfer (Conduction)
Q = k × A × ΔT / d
Heat conducted through a material of thickness d.
k = Thermal conductivity (W/mK)  |  A = Area (m²)  |  d = Thickness (m)
Watts (W)
Pump Power
P = (ρ × g × Q × H) / η
Shaft power required by a pump to deliver flow Q against head H.
ρ = 1000 kg/m³ (water)  |  g = 9.81 m/s²  |  H = Head (m)  |  η = efficiency
Watts (W)
Factor of Safety
FoS = Ultimate strength / Working load
Minimum safe ratio. Structural steel: FoS ≥ 1.67. Lifting equipment: FoS ≥ 4.0.
Ultimate strength from material datasheets  |  Working load = design load
Dimensionless (≥1.5)
❄ HVAC & Building Services Formulas
Sensible Heat Formula
Q = ṁ × Cp × ΔT
Heat to change temperature of air (no phase change). Used for AHU sizing.
ṁ = Mass flow rate (kg/s)  |  Cp = 1,005 J/kgK (air)  |  ΔT = temp rise (°C)
Watts (W)
Latent Heat (Dehumidification)
Q_lat = ṁ × hfg × Δω
Cooling energy to remove moisture from air.
hfg ≈ 2,501 kJ/kg  |  Δω = humidity ratio difference (kg/kg dry air)
Watts (W)
Air Flow from Cooling Load
Q̇ = Q_sensible / (1.2 × ΔT × Cp)
Volume flow rate of supply air needed. ΔT = supply-to-room temp difference.
Supply air temp typically 12–14°C  |  Room temp 24°C → ΔT ≈ 10°C
m³/s or L/s
COP (Coefficient of Performance)
COP = Q_useful / W_input
Efficiency of cooling or heating. Modern chillers: COP 5–8. Heat pumps: COP 3–5.
Q_useful = Cooling output (W)  |  W_input = Compressor power (W)
Dimensionless (>1)
EER to COP Conversion
COP = EER / 3.412
Convert Energy Efficiency Ratio (BTU/Wh) to COP. SEER × 0.875 ≈ EER.
EER = BTU/Wh  |  SEER = Seasonal EER (BTU/Wh)
Dimensionless
U-value (Overall Heat Transfer)
U = 1 / (Rsi + ΣR + Rso)
Overall thermal transmittance. Lower U = better insulation. Wall target: U < 0.3 W/m²K.
Rsi = inside surface resistance (0.13)  |  R = layer resistance d/k  |  Rso = outside (0.04)
W/m²K
Duct Pressure Drop
ΔP = f × (L/D) × (ρv²/2)
Darcy-Weisbach equation for duct friction loss. Typical: 1–1.5 Pa/m.
f = Friction factor  |  L = Length (m)  |  D = Diameter (m)  |  v = velocity (m/s)
Pascals (Pa)
Refrigerant Mass Flow Rate
ṁ = Q_evap / (h1 - h4)
Refrigerant flow needed for a given evaporator capacity.
h1 = Enthalpy at evaporator outlet  |  h4 = Enthalpy at evaporator inlet (kJ/kg)
kg/s
🏗 Civil & Structural Engineering Formulas
Bending Stress
σ = M × y / I
Maximum stress in a beam under bending moment M.
M = Bending moment (Nm)  |  y = Distance from neutral axis (m)  |  I = Second moment of area (m⁴)
Pa (N/m²)
Simply Supported Beam Deflection
δ_max = 5 × w × L⁴ / (384 × E × I)
Maximum midspan deflection under UDL. Limit: L/360 for live load.
w = UDL (N/m)  |  L = Span (m)  |  E = Young's modulus  |  I = Inertia (m⁴)
Metres (m)
Column Buckling (Euler)
P_cr = π² × E × I / (K × L)²
Critical buckling load for a slender column. K = effective length factor.
K = 1.0 (pinned-pinned), 0.5 (fixed-fixed), 0.7 (pinned-fixed)
Newtons (N)
Concrete Mix Design (Water-Cement Ratio)
f'c ↑ as w/c ↓    w/c ≤ 0.45 (aggressive exposure)
Lower w/c ratio = stronger, more durable concrete. Target: ≤ 0.5 for structural work.
w/c = water mass / cement mass  |  f'c = compressive strength (MPa)
Dimensionless ratio
Settlement (Terzaghi)
S = Cc/(1+e₀) × H × log(σ'f/σ'i)
Consolidation settlement of a clay layer under increased effective stress.
Cc = Compression index  |  e₀ = initial void ratio  |  H = layer thickness  |  σ = effective stress
Metres (m)
Manning's Flow Equation (Open Channel)
Q = (1/n) × A × R^(2/3) × S^(1/2)
Discharge in open channels. n = Manning roughness (0.013 for concrete).
A = Flow area (m²)  |  R = Hydraulic radius (m)  |  S = Channel slope (m/m)
m³/s
💻 PLC & Control Systems Formulas
PID Control Output
u(t) = Kp×e + Ki×∫e dt + Kd×de/dt
PID controller output. e = error = Setpoint − Process Variable.
Kp = Proportional gain  |  Ki = Integral gain  |  Kd = Derivative gain
Output (0–100%)
Analog Scaling (4–20mA)
EU = (Raw - 0) / 27648 × (EU_max - EU_min) + EU_min
Convert S7 raw AI value (0–27648) to engineering units. 4mA=0, 20mA=27648.
Raw = PLC register value  |  EU = Engineering unit (e.g. bar, °C)
Engineering units
Ziegler-Nichols PID Tuning
Kp = 0.6×Ku  |  Ti = Tu/2  |  Td = Tu/8
Starting point for PID tuning using ultimate gain Ku and oscillation period Tu.
Ku = Gain at sustained oscillation  |  Tu = Oscillation period (s) at Ku
Dimensionless / seconds
Scan Cycle Time
T_scan = T_input + T_program + T_output + T_comm
Total PLC scan time. Must be ≪ process time constant for stable control.
T_input ≈ 0.1–2ms  |  T_program = logic exec  |  T_output ≈ 0.1–2ms
Milliseconds (ms)
Motor Starting Current
I_start ≈ 6–8 × I_FLC (DOL)  |  2–3 × I_FLC (Star-Delta)
Inrush current on motor start. Star-delta reduces starting current to 1/3 of DOL.
I_FLC = Full load current  |  DOL = Direct Online  |  VFD = soft start, adjustable
Amperes (A)
VFD Speed Control
n = (120 × f) / p    f_out = n_desired × p / 120
Motor speed from frequency. VFD varies f to control speed. p = number of poles.
n = Speed (RPM)  |  f = Frequency (Hz)  |  p = Number of poles (2, 4, 6, 8)
RPM
📜 Mathematics for Engineers
Quadratic Formula
x = (-b ± √(b²-4ac)) / 2a
Roots of ax² + bx + c = 0. Discriminant b²-4ac > 0: two real roots; = 0: one root; < 0: complex.
a, b, c = coefficients of the quadratic equation
Real or complex
Pythagoras Theorem
c² = a² + b²    c = √(a² + b²)
Hypotenuse of a right triangle. Used constantly in vector resolution, impedance triangles.
a, b = legs of right triangle  |  c = hypotenuse
Same as input units
Trigonometry (SOHCAHTOA)
sin θ = O/H   cos θ = A/H   tan θ = O/A
Opposite (O), Adjacent (A), Hypotenuse (H) in a right triangle angle θ.
sin²θ + cos²θ = 1  |  tan θ = sin θ / cos θ
Dimensionless (ratio)
Laws of Logarithms
log(ab) = log a + log b  |  log(a/b) = log a - log b  |  log(aⁿ) = n log a
Used in decibel calculations, growth rates, and filter design.
log = log base 10  |  ln = natural log (base e = 2.718)
Dimensionless
Circle Area & Circumference
A = π r²    C = 2πr = πd
Area and perimeter of a circle. Used for pipe flow area, cable cross-sections.
r = radius  |  d = diameter  |  π ≈ 3.14159
Area: m²  |  Circumference: m
Unit Conversions (Key)
1 kW = 1.341 HP  |  1 bar = 100 kPa  |  1 TR = 3.517 kW  |  1 kWh = 3.6 MJ
Essential unit conversions in daily engineering practice.
1 BTU = 0.293 Wh  |  1 psi = 6.895 kPa  |  1 lbf = 4.448 N
Various
Statistics — Mean & Standard Deviation
x̄ = Σx/n    σ = √(Σ(x-x̄)²/n)
Mean (average) and standard deviation. Used in quality control and data analysis.
x = data values  |  n = number of data points  |  σ = population standard deviation
Same as data units
Derivative (Instantaneous Rate)
d(xⁿ)/dx = n×xⁿ⁻¹    d(sin x)/dx = cos x
Used in control systems (derivative action), stress analysis, and optimization.
d(eˣ)/dx = eˣ  |  d(ln x)/dx = 1/x  |  d(cos x)/dx = -sin x
Rate of change
